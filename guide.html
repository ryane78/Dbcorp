How to build a house in Islamabad: 6 simple steps

What happens, in what order, and what to check, so you can build with confidence. Written by DBCorp, a design-build construction company in Islamabad, for homeowners building in Islamabad and Rawalpindi.

Step 1 of 6 · 1–3 months

Plan and get approvals

Get the plan and the paperwork right before anyone starts digging.

  1. Check your plotMake sure the corner markers match the plot map.
  2. Test the soilThis tells the engineer how deep the foundation must go.
  3. Get two sets of drawingsArchitectural drawings for the layout, and structural drawings for the steel and concrete.
  4. Get the plan approvedFrom CDA, RDA, DHA, Bahria or your society. Never start without approval.
  5. Apply for utilitiesApply for electricity, water and gas early, because they take time.
  6. Make a budgetList every item of work, and keep 10% extra.

Check before moving on

  • Approved plan in hand
  • Contract signed, with payments linked to stages
  • Store and water ready on site

Tip

Before you choose a contractor, visit two houses they have built: one finished and one still under construction.

Step 2 of 6 · 3–5 weeks

Foundation

This is the base of your house. Once it is buried, it can’t be fixed cheaply.

  1. Mark the layoutMeasure both diagonals of each room. If they are equal, the corners are square.
  2. DigTo the depth on the drawing, usually 4–6 ft for a 1–2 storey home.
  3. Press the base firmBrick ballast is laid, watered and compacted.
  4. Treat for termitesThis should be done by a licensed pest-control firm.
  5. Pour the footingsThe steel sits on spacer blocks, and the concrete is shaken with a vibrator.
  6. Seal with DPCA damp-proof layer stops moisture rising into the walls.

Check before moving on

  • Engineer checked the steel before the pour
  • Soil filled back in layers, each pressed firm
  • DPC laid

Tip

Never let concrete be poured until your engineer has checked the steel.

Step 3 of 6 · 4–6 months

Structure and roof

The columns, walls and roof slabs form the skeleton of your house.

  1. Build columns and wallsUse good bricks, soaked in water before laying.
  2. Fix door and window framesOrder them early. They go in as the walls rise.
  3. Prepare the roof slabShuttering, steel and electrical points all go in before the concrete.
  4. Engineer checksBefore every roof pour, without exception.
  5. Pour the slab in one goStopping halfway leaves a weak line in the roof.
  6. Keep it wetWater the slab for 10–14 days, and leave the supports in for at least 14.

Check before moving on

  • Slab steel checked
  • Fan hooks and wiring pipes placed before the pour
  • Slab watered every day

Tip

Be on site the day the roof is poured. It is the most important day of the build.

Step 4 of 6 · 1–2 months

Pipes and wiring

Everything hidden inside your walls goes in now, before they are closed up.

  1. Wiring pipesGive ACs, the kitchen and the geyser their own circuits.
  2. Water pipesPressure-test them before the walls are closed.
  3. DrainsLay them on a steady slope, and run water through them to test.
  4. WaterproofingWaterproof the roof and bathrooms before any tiles go down.
  5. PlasterWork from the top floor down, and keep the plaster wet for a week.

Check before moving on

  • Water pipes tested
  • Roof and bathrooms waterproofed
  • Earthing installed

Tip

Photograph every wall before plaster, with a measuring tape in the shot. That’s your map of hidden pipes and wires, forever.

Step 5 of 6 · 4–6 months

Finishing

This is what you will see and touch every day.

  1. Choose earlyPick tiles, fittings and the kitchen early. Late choices are the biggest cause of delay.
  2. FloorsBuy 5–10% extra tiles, all from the same batch.
  3. Bathrooms and kitchenDecide where every fitting goes before tiling.
  4. Doors and windowsHang the doors after the floors are laid.
  5. PaintPaint only once the walls are completely dry.
  6. Lights, fans and ACsInstall these last, once the dusty work is done.

Check before moving on

  • Tiles tapped: no hollow sounds
  • Walls dry before painting
  • Defects list cleared

Tip

Keep a list of every small defect as you go, and get each one fixed before the workers leave.

Step 6 of 6 · 2–4 weeks

Test and move in

Test everything while the contractor is still around to fix it.

  1. RoofFill the roof with water for 72 hours, then check the ceilings below for damp.
  2. Water and drainsOpen every tap and run water through every drain.
  3. ElectricityTest every switch, socket and breaker.
  4. GasA licensed fitter checks for leaks with soapy water, never a flame.
  5. Water tanksClean them before you use the water.
  6. PaperworkCollect the completion certificate and the warranties.

Check before moving on

  • All tests passed
  • Completion certificate applied for
  • Final payment settled

Tip

Then the best part: Quran khwani, a housewarming, and your family’s first night home.

Paying your contractor

Pay only for finished work

Money should follow finished, checked work. A schedule like this keeps everyone honest.

Never pay ahead of the work.

Agree all changes in writing first.

On your site visits

8 warning signs

You don’t need to be an engineer to spot these. If you see one, ask about it straight away.

  1. Thin or light steelAsk to weigh a sample and check the stamp on the bar.
  2. Fewer cement bagsCount the bags used against the plan.
  3. Watery concreteGood concrete holds its shape. It doesn’t flow like soup.
  4. Steel touching the groundIt needs spacer blocks under it, or it will rust.
  5. Dry roof after a pourNobody is watering it. The slab must stay wet.
  6. Holes in concreteRough, hollow patches mean no vibrator was used.
  7. Cracked or uneven bricksGood bricks ring when tapped together.
  8. No pipe testingAsk to see the test before walls are closed.
